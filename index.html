Selected accomplishments across Umbra roles

  • Built Umbra's flight-software department from the ground up, hiring and managing 12 direct reports and establishing software architecture, development processes, nightly CI/CD, test infrastructure, and delivery practices.
  • Drove spacecraft platform architecture in collaboration with approximately 20 electrical and mechanical engineers, spanning flight computers, GNC, payload, radio, avionics, and ground-command interfaces.
  • Led the transition from bare-metal flight software to embedded Linux, with responsibilities spanning firmware, FPGA, Linux kernel development, and GNC model integration. Hands-on Zynq-7000 and Zynq UltraScale+ work included bootloaders, BSPs, drivers, DDR, DMA, and processor/FPGA interfaces.
  • Architected two TRL 9 spacecraft update solutions: golden-image recovery for a Zynq-7000 controller and delta/rolling bootloader, kernel, and application updates across four onboard computers, incorporating nonvolatile staging, checksum validation, redundant boot images, and automatic rollback.
  • Architected TRL 9 dual-flight-computer redundancy with watchdog supervision, onboard-computer selection logic, and automatic failover.
  • Designed fault detection, isolation, and recovery (FDIR) logic supporting selective sensor/actuator resets, GNC model restart, and a low-power sun-pointing safe mode.
  • Made hardware/software architecture and component-selection decisions for radiation-tolerant spacecraft systems, including nonvolatile-memory selection with FRAM.
  • Delivered flight software across three spacecraft blocks successfully deployed to orbit, with no mission losses attributable to flight software.
  • Developed environmental test plans spanning execution, teardown, and pre-/post-test functional validation; supported spacecraft thermal-vacuum, shock, and vibration verification.
  • Reviewed processor schematics and PCB layouts to verify pin assignments and required hardware/software interfaces.
